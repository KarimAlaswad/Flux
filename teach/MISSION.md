# Mission: System Design

## Why

I want to learn system design as a general skill. My practice ground is this workspace's codebase (Flux) — a prototype I built as a beginner. The goal is not just to understand system design concepts, but to **overhaul Flux into a well-designed system**, piece by piece, as I learn. Each lesson teaches a concept, audits Flux against it, and results in a concrete redesign plan.

## Success looks like

- I can look at any system's architecture and identify strengths, weaknesses, and design debt
- Flux, after the lesson sequence, is a genuinely well-designed system — not just "better"
- I can evaluate design trade-offs aloud with the right vocabulary
- I know when to redesign vs when to patch, and I have the skills to do either
- The system design skill transfers: I can apply it to future projects, not just this one

## Constraints

- Absolute beginner to formal system design — but I built Flux, so I have practical intuition
- No time pressure — depth over speed
- Flux is the practice ground: every lesson produces a tangible improvement to it

## Out of scope

- Cloud infrastructure (AWS, Kubernetes, etc.)
- Distributed consensus protocols (Paxos, Raft, CRDTs)
- Frontend performance optimization
- Low-level networking (TCP internals, BGP)
